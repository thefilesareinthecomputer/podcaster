# spine: when the volunteer gauge and the county sensor disagree, which number does the town act on?
[S1] The Little Pell volunteer station has logged rain by hand since nineteen sixty-one. Last month the volunteer wrote down one and three tenths inches. The county sensor, four hundred feet away, recorded nothing at all.
[S2] Wait. Four hundred feet is nothing in a rainstorm, so one of those two instruments is broken, and the cheap answer is always that the hand-written one is broken.
[S1] Except the tipping bucket on the county unit had a wasp nest in it. Two seasons of that, and every reading it sent to the state got a Q C flag nobody read.
[S2] So the broken one was the one with the firmware... and the town spent two budget cycles arguing that a retired schoolteacher could not read a graduated cylinder.
[S1] Ninety-one percent of the volunteer's monthly totals match the state's regional model within a tenth of an inch. The sensor's match rate over the same stretch - sixty-four percent.
[S2] Then the town has its answer, and it is not the one that cost money. Read both, publish both, and when they split, send somebody out to look at the gauge.
